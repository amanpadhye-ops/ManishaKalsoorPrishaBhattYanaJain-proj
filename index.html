IntelliConvene

The event operating system that helps organisers run events, not just publish them.

A consulting-style diagnosis of the platform built by IntelliCredence, covering what it is, who it serves, how it speaks to them and where the brand is strong or exposed.

A1

What is IntelliConvene?

IntelliConvene is an event-management platform developed by IntelliCredence and positioned as an “event operating system”. It is built around the volunteer organiser, someone who runs a community meetup alongside a full-time job, and aims to give venues, speakers, sponsors and caterers one shared place to coordinate.

Its clearest endorsement so far comes from Collabnix, a developer community that reports eight months of collaboration with the platform and is now moving away from Meetup.com.

In short, it aims to take an organiser from “I have an idea for a meetup” to the event actually happening, without needing five separate tools along the way.

A2

What problem does it solve?

Demand side

Publishing is easy. Running is hard.

A volunteer organiser can publish an event within minutes but struggles to run it. A Collabnix organiser describes restarting the venue search for every event, having no direction on food sponsors, handling speaker calls for papers without a central system and losing contacts across email threads.

Coordination ends up scattered across spreadsheets, inboxes and messaging groups.

Supply side

Capacity without a route to market

Service providers have limited visibility. Venues and caterers may hold unused capacity yet lack a credible route to community events.

IntelliConvene addresses both sides by acting as a single unified layer that connects supply with demand.

A3

Major features and capabilities

The core features most relevant to the volunteer organiser, as listed by the Collabnix team.

Venue discovery and management

Find and manage venues without restarting the search for every event.

Speaker hub

Call-for-papers management in one place.

Sponsor coordination

Tiered packages and a clear route to funding.

Catering logistics

Connect events with caterers who have spare capacity.

Centralised communication

One channel in place of scattered threads and groups.

Live analytics and insights

Real-time tracked data on how an event is performing.

Additional capabilities

Customisable templates, integrated ticketing and payments, automated email campaigns, custom event websites, team roles and post-event surveys. Sponsors can select tiered packages that offer benefits such as logo placement, complimentary passes, attendee data and post-event analytics.

A4

Stakeholders

The platform serves six stakeholders, with the volunteer organiser at the centre of the ecosystem. Everyone except sponsors uses it free of charge.

Organisers

The entry point and primary decision makers. Their adoption brings everyone else onto the platform.

Attendees

Supply the audience that gives events their value. They use the platform free of charge.

Speakers

Supply the content. A central call-for-papers hub replaces scattered email threads.

Sponsors

The revenue stakeholder. Local technology brands seeking developer attention are the natural fit.

Venues

Part of the strategic differentiator. They hold the space organisers most struggle to secure.

Caterers

Also part of the differentiator. They hold capacity and need a credible route to community events.

A5

Current brand proposition

Speaking to everyone at once

The current proposition addresses all audiences simultaneously. The homepage presents “one platform, endless paths”, and the stated mission is to simplify event management and create meaningful connections through an all-in-one platform.

The idea that sets it apart

The sharper idea already exists in the founding community’s voice. Most event platforms help organisers publish events but not run them. That publish-versus-run distinction is what separates IntelliConvene from the rest.

A6

Current communication

The website

Communication is concentrated in owned channels, and the volunteer organiser appears in only a few of them. The website is broad and organised by stakeholder role across the whole ecosystem, so it does not directly address community organisers and helpers.

The community voice

This is where the brand’s niche emerges. An organiser’s origin-story article on DEV, a publishing platform for software developers, links to the first host on the platform and invites readers into a Slack community.

Collabnix has also published several articles on how tech meetups are getting an event OS upgrade. This shows a positioning built on seeding the idea rather than direct promotion.

A7

What is working and not working

Working

  • The publish-versus-run insight resonates with organisers’ lived experience.
  • Free entry limits the volunteer’s risk to time alone.
  • The peer-led origin story is more credible than conventional advertising.
  • A real community shows live analytics and proof without hesitation.

Not working

  • The website does not speak directly to the volunteer’s situation, which weakens relevance.
  • Headline figures confuse the audience and add to appearance without evidence.
  • Proof is narrow. Credibility rests on one community and no independent reviews support it.
