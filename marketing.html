Section B · Phase 1

Marketing Communication

How IntelliConvene reaches its audience today, the strategies behind that reach, who it is aimed at and how well the communication performs.

ChannelsStrategiesTarget audienceStrengths & weaknesses
B1

Current marketing channels

Owned channel · Website

The homepage uses role-based sign-up journeys along with a user testimonial, with pricing features written consecutively.

Community content · DEV

DEV is a publishing platform used mainly by software developers. A Collabnix organiser announced the platform in his own voice and attached the link to the first event hosted on it.

Community messaging · Slack

Readers of the website and community articles are invited to join the IntelliCredence Slack workspace. This moves audiences from one-way reading into two-way conversation with the team. Collabnix adds reach with 17,000+ members across Slack and Discord.

Partner communities and products

Collabnix publishes articles promoting upcoming technology meetups. Each event hosted on IntelliConvene also acts as a live product demonstration for attendees, speakers and sponsors.

LinkedIn · primary social channel

Used to share company updates, promote events, announce partnerships and engage professional communities.

B2

Marketing strategies implemented

Community-led launch

The platform and website were built alongside a developing community, and grow as the number of events on the site increases.

Free entry for most stakeholders

Organisers, speakers, venues and caterers use the platform and its demonstration for free. The only required payments come from sponsors.

Category positioning

Framing it as an event operating system sets it against tools that only publish events. The market splits into tools that help organisers publish and a platform that helps them run.

B3

Target audience

Primary segment

Event organisers

This covers college and university committees, corporate event and L&D managers, conference and community organisers and independent planners. They make the buying decision, and their adoption brings speakers, sponsors, venues, caterers and attendees onto the platform.

Demographics

Aged 20 to 40. Student leaders running fests and clubs, or early-to-mid-career professionals in marketing, HR and administration at colleges, SMEs and mid-sized corporates.

Geographics

Metro and tier 2 and 3 cities, where digital adoption is rising.

Psychographics

  • Time-poor multitaskers juggling several roles on tight budgets.
  • Pragmatic, wanting tools that simply work rather than complex enterprise suites.
  • Digitally fluent and at ease with community channels such as Discord and Slack, which Collabnix runs.

Behavioural

They coordinate through spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups and separate vendors, run recurring event cycles, hunt for sponsors and respond to free trials.

Secondary segments

Sponsors, speakers, venues, caterers and Gen Z or millennial attendees.

B4

Communication strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  1. Clear product positioning. The “Operating System for Modern Events” proposition communicates the ambition to bring event activities and stakeholders onto one platform.
  2. Multi-stakeholder appeal. Communication identifies six distinct groups, namely organisers, attendees, speakers, sponsors, venues and caterers, giving the brand several segments to engage.
  3. Partnership-led visibility. Collaborations with technology communities and professional networks introduce the platform to relevant audiences.

Weaknesses

  1. Limited awareness and reliance on one channel. Visibility comes mainly through professional networking and technology community partnerships, which may limit reach to individual attendees and wider audiences.
  2. Broad messaging. One strategy for several groups risks diluting the message, since organisers, sponsors and attendees are motivated differently.
  3. Insufficient social proof. Testimonials and performance claims exist, but detailed case studies, event success stories and measurable outcomes would build more trust.
  4. Inconsistent stakeholder communication. The website names six stakeholder groups while newer LinkedIn communication introduces exhibitors as a seventh, so messaging could be more consistent.
